📖 WRITING FOCUS
Voice and dialogue that reveal character
Narrative craft · builds toward your Module 1 project, The Bard’s Hall
This week you watched Odysseus become the teller of his own adventures, and you heard Polyphemus, Aeolus, and Circe each speak in a way that revealed who they were. In Lesson 5.03 you practiced writing dialogue that shows rather than tells. Now you draft a real scene from your Bard’s Hall story using that same technique.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Write a scene (200 to 300 words) from your Bard’s Hall tale in which at least two characters speak to each other. Use dialogue to reveal something about each character, rather than explaining it directly. This scene can extend a piece you have already drafted (your opening, your world, or your setting) or start something new.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
What can your characters say, and how can they say it, that shows the reader who they are without ever stating it outright?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Choose a moment in your Bard’s Hall story where two characters need something from each other, or where one is hiding something from the other. Tension makes dialogue easier to write well.
2
Write the scene mostly through dialogue. Let word choice, not narration, reveal whether a character is confident, nervous, cunning, or proud, the way Odysseus’s and Polyphemus’s own words revealed them this week.
3
Punctuate every line of dialogue correctly: commas and end punctuation inside quotation marks, a new line for each new speaker, and correct capitalization when a tag interrupts a sentence.
4
Work in at least two of this week’s Word Study words (subtlety, valiant, fraud, counsel, plight, renowned), used accurately. Reread once and ask: could a reader tell what each character wants, just from what they say?
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
The scene includes dialogue between at least two characters, and the dialogue reveals something about each speaker.
☐
Dialogue is punctuated correctly throughout (quotation marks, commas, capitalization, new line per speaker).
☐
At least two of this week’s Word Study words are used accurately.
☐
The writing is 200 to 300 words.
📊 HOW IT’S SCORED
Criteria Full credit Partial Pts
Dialogue reveals character
5 pts		 Word choice, not narration, makes each speaker’s personality or state of mind clear. Dialogue is present but leans on the narrator to explain what it reveals. 5
Dialogue punctuation
3 pts		 Quotation marks, commas, and capitalization are correct throughout. One or two punctuation errors appear in the dialogue. 3
Word Study & length
2 pts		 At least two Word Study words used accurately; 200 to 300 words. Fewer than two Word Study words, or off the length target. 2
Total: 10 pts
