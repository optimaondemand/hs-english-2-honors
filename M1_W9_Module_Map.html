🎯 THE QUESTION THIS MODULE ASKS
What does a community owe its king, and what does a king owe his community?
This is the week the whole poem has been building toward, and the week the question finally gets answered. Odysseus reclaims his hall by force, but the poem does not let the story end there: Penelope makes him prove who he is before she accepts him back, and the community of Ithaca has to find a way to live with a king who has just killed dozens of its young men. Read Books 21 through 24 watching for both halves of the question: what Odysseus is owed after everything he has survived, and what he now owes the people he rules.
🏆 THIS WEEK IS YOUR CAPSTONE WEEK
Your final Bard’s Hall project, the finished narrative and its recorded telling, is due this week. There is no Quick Write in Week 9. Instead, every lesson this week is built to help you revise, polish, and prepare to perform the story you have been building since Week 1. Go to the Bard’s Hall module project page for the full submission instructions, the rubric, and the due date.
📖 THIS WEEK’S READING — YOUR OWN COPY (FAGLES)
Books 21–24
Scene anchors to watch for: Penelope sets the contest of the great bow; the suitors try and fail to string it; the beggar strings the bow and reveals himself as Odysseus; the slaying of the suitors, with Telemachus and the loyal servants fighting at his side; Penelope’s test of the secret of their marriage bed; Odysseus’s reunion with his father Laertes; the suitors’ families arm for revenge; the goddess halts the fighting and peace is restored to Ithaca. This is a four-book stretch to close out the epic; see the “How to Read the Odyssey” guide if you need to catch up.
📋 WHAT’S IN THIS WEEK
Lesson
9.01 · The Contest of the Bow
Books 21–22: only Odysseus can string the great bow. He reveals himself and, with Telemachus and his loyal servants, defeats the suitors. Justice and vengeance, and what it costs to take a hall back by force.
Lesson
9.02 · The House Restored
Books 23–24: Penelope tests the stranger with the secret of their marriage bed and finally knows him. Odysseus is reunited with his father, the threatened feud with the suitors’ families is halted, and peace returns to Ithaca.
Lesson
9.03 · Revise and Perform
A writing-technique lesson: how to revise and polish a finished narrative, and how to prepare it to be shared aloud. Direct preparation for your final Bard’s Hall submission.
Grammar & Word Study
9.GV · Proofreading
A final proofreading pass and the commonly confused words that trip up even careful writers, plus this week’s Word Study words from Books 21–24.
VR
VR Activity 9.01 · Premiere Night
A solo activity performing your finished Bard’s Hall telling for an audience, the way a bard would premiere a story in a great hall.
VR
VR Activity 9.02 · The House Set Right
A solo activity staging what a restored, peaceful household looks like after a long absence, closing out the module’s central image of a king and his community.
