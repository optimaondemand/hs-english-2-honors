Solo VR Activity 8.01
The Hall Before the Storm
Solo VR Activity · Week 8 · Completion-graded
Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
This week’s reading holds a room full of tension that has not yet broken: a disguised king absorbing insult after insult, a hall full of people who do not know what is coming. In this activity, working solo, you will stage that exact feeling, a room on the edge of something, in space. This is direct preparation for the tensest scene in your own Bard’s Hall draft, the one Lesson 8.03 asked you to pace with care.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Working solo, choose or arrange a space to represent your Bard’s Hall scene at its most tense moment, the one just before your climax.
2
Place or arrange objects, lighting, or position to show what is about to happen without giving it away, the way the poem holds back the moment Odysseus finally reveals himself.
3
Speak or narrate one to two sentences describing the moment as if you were pausing a scene right before it breaks, in the voice of your story’s narrator.
4
Name out loud one specific detail you are deliberately withholding from your reader in this scene, and why holding it back matters.
5
Save your capture and submit it, following the submission instructions below.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A short (about 60 to 90 second) recording or capture of your staged, tense-before-the-break moment, in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a screen or video recording with narration, an audio recording paired with a few photos or sketches, or a short written script with a brief description if recording is not possible. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
The staged scene clearly represents a moment of rising tension from your own Bard’s Hall story.
☐
One specific withheld detail is named out loud, along with a reason for holding it back.
☐
The narration is one to two sentences, delivered in the story’s own voice.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can support it by asking students to name their story’s climax out loud in one sentence before they begin staging, so the tense moment they build actually points somewhere. Common sticking point: students stage a generic tense room without tying it to their own draft; redirect them to build the specific scene their story needs, not a stock spooky setting.
