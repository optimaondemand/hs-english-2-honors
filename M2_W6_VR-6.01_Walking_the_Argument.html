Solo VR Activity 6.01
Walking the Argument
Solo VR Activity · Week 6 · Completion-graded
Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
A brief lives on paper as six labeled sections. In this activity, working solo, you will turn that structure into a physical space: six stations you can actually walk between, one for each move of your brief (Issue, Verdict, Argument, Counterclaim, Response, Conclusion). Walking the structure and speaking it aloud, station by station, is a different kind of check than rereading a page; it will show you immediately if a move in your outline is thin, out of order, or missing entirely.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Working solo, mark out six stations in a row, in a space you have available: six spots on the floor, six sticky notes on a wall, six chairs, or a modern equivalent. Label them, in order: Issue, Verdict, Argument, Counterclaim, Response, Conclusion. (If you have more than one Argument in your Quick Write 14 outline, you may add an extra station for each additional argument, kept in order between Verdict and Counterclaim.)
2
Walk the stations once, in order, without your outline in hand. At each one, speak one sentence aloud naming that station’s job, not your actual case yet: for example, standing at Counterclaim, say something like, “This is where I state the strongest case against my own verdict, fairly, not as a strawman.”
3
Now walk the stations a second time, with your Quick Write 14 outline nearby. At each station, speak your actual content aloud: your real Issue, your real Verdict, and so on, in your own words, not read word for word off the page.
4
Notice any station where you hesitate, go silent, or find yourself saying much less than at the others. That is the part of your brief most likely to need more work before you draft it in full next week.
5
Record your second walkthrough (Step 3), save it, and submit it, following the instructions below.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A short (about 90 second to 2 minute) recording or capture of your second walkthrough (your real content, not the “job description” pass), in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a screen or video recording, an audio recording with a few photos of your stations, or a written transcript of what you said at each station if recording is not possible. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
All six stations (Issue, Verdict, Argument(s), Counterclaim, Response, Conclusion) appear, in order.
☐
The recording shows the second walkthrough: your actual case at each station, not just a description of each station’s job.
☐
The Counterclaim station states a real opposing case, not a strawman, and the Response station answers it directly.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can support it by having students set up their six stations before they try speaking at any of them, so the physical structure is fixed first. Common sticking point: students rush past the Counterclaim and Response stations, giving them only a sentence each while the earlier stations run long; redirect students to notice this imbalance themselves during the first, silent walkthrough, before they record.
