Solo VR Activity · Week 4 · Completion-graded
Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
A story written on a page and a story told out loud are not quite the same thing. In this activity, working solo, you will read the scene you wrote for Quick Write 4 aloud to an audience, real or imagined, and pay attention to how your own long comparison lands when it is spoken rather than silently read. This is a direct rehearsal for the recitation ahead and for the eventual telling of your finished Bard’s Hall tale.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Working solo, set up a space where you can speak comfortably to an audience: a room, an outdoor spot, or a modern equivalent such as facing a phone camera or a family member.
2
Read your Quick Write 4 scene aloud once straight through, at a natural pace, the way you would if no one had told you it needed to be performed.
3
Read it a second time, and this time slow down deliberately for your epic simile, letting the extended comparison breathe instead of rushing through it.
4
Narrate one sentence about what was different between the first reading and the second, once you slowed down for the comparison on purpose.
5
Save your capture and submit it, following the submission instructions below.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A short (about 60 to 90 second) recording or capture of your second, slowed-down reading, in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a video or audio recording, an audio recording paired with a written copy of your scene, or a written note describing the pacing change if recording is not possible. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
The reading includes the Quick Write 4 scene and its epic simile in full.
☐
A clear, deliberate pace change is audible or described during the epic simile.
☐
One sentence names what changed between the first and second reading.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can support it by reading one sentence aloud twice, once flat and once slowed down for emphasis, so students hear the difference before trying it themselves. Common sticking point: students read both passes at the same speed and cannot identify a difference; encourage them to exaggerate the slow-down more than feels natural on the second reading, since a felt difference is what the completion criteria require.
