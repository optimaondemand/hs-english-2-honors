🎮 DESIGN FOCUS
Bringing Quarter 2 together into one milestone
The Designer’s Table · Q2 milestone: prototype v2 + design rationale
Oedipus offers a designer two ideas at once: a world where fate and personal agency are tangled together in a way no character can fully untangle, and a structure where one early act compounds, scene by scene, until it cannot be escaped. Decide what your own game owes to that lens, in writing, whether you use it directly or set it aside on purpose. Then put your revised prototype and your reasoning into a single design document that explains, to a stranger, how your mechanics actually carry your theme.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Apply the Oedipus design lens (fate versus agency, and compounding consequence) to your game, or reject it deliberately with reasons, in writing. Finish prototype v2, incorporating your Sprint 6 and Sprint 7 revisions. Write a 400 to 600 word design rationale, framed explicitly as a design document, not a literary essay, explaining how your mechanics carry your design question and citing your own rules and playtest evidence as proof. Get an AI red-team critique of that rationale and respond to it in writing.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If you had to prove that your mechanics, not just your setting or your story, carry your game’s argument, what specific rule would you point to as your evidence?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Apply the Oedipus design lens, or reject it with reasons. Consider two ideas the play offers a designer: fate versus agency (how much of an outcome a character or player actually controls, versus how much was set before the game or the play ever began), and compounding consequence (how one early fact or choice closes off options as it goes). Decide, in writing, whether your game will build one of these directly into its rules or deliberately set it aside, and explain your reasoning either way. Rejecting the lens is a legitimate design decision as long as you argue for it.
2
Finish prototype v2. Fold in everything you revised in Sprint 6 (consequence and feedback) and Sprint 7 (playtest revisions) into one coherent, playable version. Photograph it or describe it in enough detail that a reader could picture exactly how it plays.
3
Write the design rationale (400–600 words). This is a design document, not a literary essay. It does not analyze the play. It explains, citing specific rules from your own game and specific evidence from your two playtest logs, how your mechanics carry your design question. Point to an actual rule and an actual moment from a playtest as your proof, not to a general description of your theme.
4
Get an AI red-team critique. Give an AI tool your design rationale and ask it to argue directly that one of your mechanics contradicts your stated theme. Read its full critique.
5
Respond in writing. Answer the AI’s critique directly: agree and explain the revision you will make, or disagree and explain, with reasoning about your own rules, why the critique misreads your design.
6
Log your AI use. Add one complete row to your AI-Use Log for the red-team step: what you asked, the critique it gave you, and how you responded.
⚠️ AI-USE REMINDER
An AI critique of your rationale is a test of your argument’s strength, not a final judgment on your design. Answer it the way you would answer a real reader: with specific reasoning about your own rules and evidence, not a general restatement of what you already believed.

💡 ABOUT THIS MILESTONE
This is a Tier 1/2 completion milestone, like every sprint before it. The design rationale should be genuinely thought through, but it does not need the finish or polish of a final product yet. The Designer’s Table carries a single graded Tier 3 defense, later, in Quarter 4, once the game has gone through a full year of playtesting and revision.
