🎮 DESIGN FOCUS
Only real play tells you what your rules actually do
The Designer’s Table · Q2 Systems & Balance · feeds your Q2 milestone
Everything you have built so far has been tested only by you and by an AI simulation, and neither of those is a substitute for a real, independent player. This sprint puts your prototype in front of people who did not design it and cannot read your mind about what you meant. What they get confused by, what drags, and what genuinely works is data no simulation can give you. This is also the sprint where you learn the sharpest limit on AI in this whole project: it can cold-read your rules for clarity, but it cannot tell you whether your game is actually fun or fair. Only a real playtest can answer that.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Recruit one to three real testers (family, friends, peers, or a recorded session, whichever fits your situation) and run your game with them. Keep a full playtest log of what you were testing, what broke or confused or dragged, and what worked. Separately, have an AI tool cold-read your written rules as a confused new player, and compare its confusion to what your real testers actually experienced. Revise your game based on what your human testers flagged.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Where did a real player get stuck in a way your AI cold-read never predicted, and where did the two agree, and what does that tell you about what AI can and cannot see in a game?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Recruit one to three testers. Family members, friends, or peers all work. If no in-person tester is available, a recorded session where you narrate the play works too, and a signed tester note is acceptable evidence of the session for async equity.
2
Run the game. Hand your tester the rules and prototype, and let them play with as little help from you as possible. Watch, and resist the urge to explain away confusion in the moment; a rule that needs your live explanation is a rule that is not finished yet.
3
Keep the playtest log. Record five things: who your testers were and how you know them, what you were testing this cycle, what broke, confused, or dragged, what worked well, and the changes you made as a result.
4
Get an AI cold read. Give an AI tool your written rules with no extra explanation and ask it to play the role of a confused new player, listing everything it finds unclear or ambiguous.
5
Fix only what both flagged. Compare the AI’s list of confusions to what your real testers actually struggled with. Revise the rules where the two agree. Where the AI raised a concern no real tester experienced, note it and leave that rule as is; a confusion that never actually confused a person is not a confirmed problem yet.
6
Log your AI use. Add one complete row to your AI-Use Log for the cold-read step: what you asked, what confusions it listed, and which ones your real testers also confirmed.
⚠️ AI-USE REMINDER
AI can tell you where your rules are unclear. It cannot tell you whether your game is fun or fair; only real people, actually playing, can answer that. This sprint is built to make that limit concrete: if the AI and your testers disagree, trust your testers.
