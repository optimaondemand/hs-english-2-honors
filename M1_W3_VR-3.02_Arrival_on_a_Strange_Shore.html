Solo VR Activity 3.02
Arrival on a Strange Shore
Solo VR Activity · Week 3 · Completion-graded
Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
Odysseus washes ashore exhausted, in a place he does not recognize, and has to decide how to face it. In this activity you will step into that same first moment: arriving somewhere unfamiliar and noticing what tells you, right away, what kind of place you have found.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Working solo, choose or enter a space you have not spent much time in before, either the setting you built in Activity 3.01 seen for the “first time,” or any other unfamiliar space available to you.
2
Imagine you are arriving there for the first time, tired and uncertain, the way Odysseus arrives on the Phaeacian shore, and take note of the very first three things you notice.
3
Decide, based on those first impressions, whether this place feels welcoming, dangerous, or somewhere in between, and why.
4
Record a short walkthrough narrating your first three impressions in order, and the judgment you formed from them, the way a stranger arriving somewhere new would think it through.
5
Save your capture and submit it, following the submission instructions below.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A short (about 60 to 90 second) recording or capture of your arrival walkthrough, in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a screen or video recording with narration, an audio recording paired with a few photos or sketches, or a short written description with images if recording is not possible. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
The recording names three specific first impressions of the space, in order.
☐
The recording states a judgment about the place (welcoming, dangerous, or in between) and explains why.
☐
The walkthrough is framed as a first arrival, not a familiar, already-known space.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can support it by reminding students that first impressions are built from small, ordinary details (sound, light, how people move) rather than grand statements. Common sticking point: students jump straight to a verdict (“it felt scary”) without naming the specific details that led there; redirect them to name the three details first, then the judgment.
