🎮 DESIGN FOCUS
The mechanic and the win condition are the argument
The Designer’s Table · core-loop sketch + prototype v0
Your world is declared and your first research is logged. Now you decide what a player actually does, over and over, in your game (the core mechanic), and exactly what causes them to win or lose (the win condition). Together these two choices are your game’s procedural argument: whatever they reward is what your game claims matters. Then you build a rough, paper version anyone could sit down and try.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Draft your game’s core mechanic and win condition, grounded in your declared world. Ask an AI tool to propose three candidate mechanics, then select the one that fits your world and design question best, explaining in your own words why you cut the other two. Finally, build a rough paper prototype (version 0) using whatever materials you have on hand, good enough for a real person to try.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If a player did only one thing over and over in your game, what would it be, and what would that one thing be quietly arguing?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Draft your core mechanic. Describe, in a few sentences, the one central action a player repeats most often in your game (moving pieces, trading, drawing and playing cards, negotiating, rolling and responding, or something else). Ground it in your researched world; a real detail from your world should shape how this action works.
2
Draft your win condition. State exactly what causes a player to win or lose. Then name, in a sentence, what that condition rewards and what it punishes (patience over speed, cooperation over hoarding, risk over caution, or some other pairing). This naming is your game’s argument in plain words.
3
Ask AI for three mechanics. Describe your world and design question to an AI tool and ask it to propose three different candidate mechanics. Read all three carefully.
4
Select and explain your cut. Choose the mechanic (or a combination) that fits your world and design question best. In your own words, explain why you are not using the other two. This reasoning must be yours, not a restatement of anything the AI said about itself.
5
Build prototype v0. Using paper, cards, dice, tokens, or any material you have on hand, build a rough, playable-enough version of your core mechanic and win condition. It does not need art or polish yet; it needs to be something a real person could actually sit down and try. Photograph or clearly describe it in your document.
6
Log your AI use. Add one complete row to your AI-Use Log for the three-mechanics step: what you asked, what it gave you, how you evaluated the three options, and what you chose and why.
⚠️ AI-USE REMINDER
An AI tool can suggest mechanics, but it cannot tell you whether one is right for your world or your argument. That judgment, and the explanation of your cut, has to be your own reasoning, not a summary of the AI’s pitch for itself.
