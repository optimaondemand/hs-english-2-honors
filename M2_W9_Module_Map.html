🎯 THE QUESTION THIS MODULE ASKS
When a community suffers, what does it owe its king, and what does the king owe it?
You have spent eight weeks building a case, and the play itself has already shown you every scene it has to show. This final week is not about new evidence. It is about standing before the tribunal you have been preparing for since Week 4, delivering the argument you have drafted and revised, and then stepping back to ask what the trial itself has taught you that the play alone could not.
🏆 THIS WEEK IS YOUR CAPSTONE WEEK
Your final Trial of Oedipus submission, the polished advocate’s brief and its recorded closing argument, is due this week. There is no Quick Write and no combined grammar and vocabulary lesson in Week 9. Instead, every lesson and activity this week is built to help you finish, polish, deliver, and reflect on the case you have been building since Week 1. Go to the Trial of Oedipus project page for the full submission instructions, the rubric, and the due date.
📋 WHAT’S IN THIS WEEK
Lesson
9.01 · The Verdict and the City
A synthesis lesson: what the trial itself teaches that the play alone could not. Revisits Oedipus’s self-sentencing, Creon’s measured rule, and the closing chorus’s warning, and leaves both standards of judgment, deed and intent, genuinely open.
Lesson
9.02 · Polish and Delivery
A final-pass writing lesson: a concrete checklist for proofreading your brief, and how to prepare and deliver your closing argument aloud, so the final sentence actually lands.
VR
VR Activity 9.01 · The Tribunal
The capstone activity: convene the Tribunal of Thebes in the space you have been staking out since Week 4, and deliver your recorded closing argument before the bench.
VR
VR Activity 9.02 · The City Healed
A closing reflection: stage the city the day the plague lifts, and answer the module’s essential question in your own words, with one line of evidence from the play.
💪 BEFORE YOU BEGIN

Open your Module 2 Organizer one last time. Look back over eight weeks of Evidence Bank entries, your standard-of-judgment decision, and your developing verdict. This is the week all of it becomes your final brief and your spoken closing argument. Start with Lesson 9.01.
