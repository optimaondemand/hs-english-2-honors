🎯 THE QUESTION THIS WEEK SHARPENS
What does a community do when its king may be the source of its own sickness?
Last week Oedipus swore to find and punish whoever polluted Thebes. This week he summons the one man in the city said to know the truth: the blind prophet Tiresias. What Tiresias says back will turn Oedipus’s own curse against him, and it will ask you, as a future advocate in the Trial of Oedipus, to start weighing evidence rather than taking sides on instinct.
📖 THIS WEEK’S READING — PUBLIC DOMAIN, LINKED FREELY
Scene landmark: the Tiresias confrontation and the chorus’s response
Read (or reread) the scene where Oedipus summons the blind prophet Tiresias, and the ode the chorus sings once Tiresias has gone. You can read the full play for free and legally at Project Gutenberg. Look for the scene by what happens in it, not by a line number.

Lesson
2.01 · The Blind Man Who Sees
The Tiresias confrontation: the summons, the refusal, the accusation, Oedipus’s rage, and the riddling exit prophecy. Sight and blindness as the play’s central irony.
Lesson

The chorus’s role in this stretch: citizens torn between a prophet’s authority and their king’s record. A first look at your future jury.
Lesson

Writing technique: what counts as evidence, quoting versus paraphrasing, and stating evidence fairly, even the evidence that hurts your own case.

How to fold a quotation into your own sentence grammatically, plus this week’s Word Study words from the Tiresias scene.

VR

VR
Solo VR Activity 2.02 · Hearing Both Sides
