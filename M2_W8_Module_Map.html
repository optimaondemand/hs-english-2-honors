🔔 DUE THIS WEEK · YOUR Q2 RECITATION
Your Q2 Recitation is due this week. You chose your passage back in Week 4 (the priest’s plague-plea or the closing chorus) and have been practicing it ever since. Go to the Recitation Q2 — Say It From Memory assignment page at the module level to review the directions, rubric, and submission steps, and record your recitation before the deadline. Do not let the revision work below crowd it out; block real time for it early in the week.
🎯 THE QUESTION THIS WEEK SHARPENS
Once you know what you believe about Oedipus, how do you make a Council actually believe it too, first on the page, and then out loud?
You have a full draft of your brief. That is not the same as a finished one. This week you turn a critical eye on your own writing, checking whether your reasoning actually holds, whether every claim is backed by real evidence, and whether your tone sounds like an advocate the Council should trust rather than one it should dismiss. Then you take your strongest material and learn to say it: a short, spoken closing argument, the same muscle your recitation has been training all module.
📖 THIS WEEK’S READING
No new reading this week.
You have already read the whole play and gathered your evidence. This week is entirely about what you do with it: revising the case you have already built, and rehearsing how you will say it aloud. If you want to check a passage against your memory while you revise, the full text remains free at Project Gutenberg, trans. F. Storr.
⚖️ YOUR MODULE PROJECT — THE TRIAL OF OEDIPUS
Quick Write 16 asks for your full draft: every section of the brief, in full prose, from the Issue through the Conclusion, plus a Works Cited listing the play and at least one researched source. It is the eighth and final feeder, and it is what your polished brief and recorded closing argument, the module project due next week, will be built from. Revise it hard this week; next week is for polish and performance, not first drafts.
📋 WHAT’S IN THIS WEEK
Lesson
8.01 · Revising the Argument
Three passes over your own draft: does the logic actually hold, is every claim backed by analyzed evidence, and does the tone sound like an advocate rather than someone who has already decided the debate is beneath them.
Lesson
8.02 · The Closing Argument
Turning your brief’s strongest material into a spoken closing, 60 to 90 seconds: your verdict, your best argument, the counterclaim dispatched, and the ask. Rehearsed now, recorded as part of next week’s project.
Grammar & Word Study
8.GV · Cutting to Strength
Conciseness as a revision skill: cutting redundant pairs, empty intensifiers, and nominalizations without losing an argument, plus this week’s Word Study words.
Composition
Quick Write 16 · Full Draft
The complete brief, every section in full prose, plus a Works Cited. Feeder 8 of 8 toward the Trial of Oedipus.
Recitation
Recitation Q2 — due this week
Your final recording of the passage you have practiced since Week 4. See the Recitation Q2 assignment page at the module level for the full directions and rubric.
VR
Solo VR Activity 8.01 · The Full Rehearsal
Deliver your full closing argument standing in your staged tribunal space, recorded, start to finish.
VR
Solo VR Activity 8.02 · Hostile Questions
Post three challenge questions an opposing advocate would ask, and answer each aloud in under thirty seconds, recorded.
💪 BEFORE YOU BEGIN

Open your full draft alongside your Module 2 Organizer, and put your Recitation Q2 due date somewhere you will actually see it. This is a heavier week; start with Lesson 8.01 and pace the two lessons, the revision itself, and the recitation across several days rather than one sitting.
