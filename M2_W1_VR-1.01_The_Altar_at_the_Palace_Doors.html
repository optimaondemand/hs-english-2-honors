Solo VR Activity 1.01
The Altar at the Palace Doors
Solo VR Activity · Week 1 · Completion-graded
Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
Oedipus the King opens on a single, striking image: a crowd of suppliants sitting on the steps of the palace, holding olive branches, silently begging their king to save them. In this activity, working solo, you will stage that exact image and speak from inside it, once as a suppliant and once as the king who steps out to meet them, so you feel from the inside what it means for a whole community to kneel at one person’s door.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Working solo, choose or arrange a space to represent the steps of a great public building: a palace entrance, a courthouse, a set of steps or a doorway, or a modern equivalent.
2
Arrange a few objects to stand in for the olive branches wrapped in wool that the suppliants carry, anything that can represent a plea held in the hands.
3
Take the suppliant’s posture (kneeling or seated, branch in hand) and speak one sentence aloud, in your own words, describing what your community has lost to the plague and what you need from your king.
4
Switch roles. Standing where the king would stand, speak one sentence responding to the suppliant, the way Oedipus does at the play’s opening: acknowledging the plea directly, before any solution is offered.
5
Save your capture and submit it, following the submission instructions below.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A short (about 60 to 90 second) recording or capture of your staged altar scene, in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a screen or video recording with narration, an audio recording paired with a few photos or sketches, or a short written script with a brief description if recording is not possible. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
The scene shows both the suppliant’s plea and the king’s response.
☐
The suppliant’s line names something specific the community has lost or fears.
☐
The king’s line acknowledges the plea directly, before offering any solution.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can support it by modeling both postures quickly (kneeling as a suppliant, then standing as the king, in a 30-second demo) before students try it themselves. Common sticking point: students jump straight to the king offering a fix instead of first simply acknowledging the suppliant’s plea; redirect them to the completion criteria above, which requires acknowledgment before any solution.
