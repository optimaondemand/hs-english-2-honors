Solo VR Activity 7.02
What the Room Doesn’t Know
Solo VR Activity · Week 7 · Completion-graded
Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
In Book 16, Odysseus and Telemachus sit together in Eumaeus’s hut holding a secret the suitors have no idea exists: a plan against them, forming in the same room the suitors will later walk into unsuspecting. In this activity, working solo, you will build a space that holds a hidden piece of information the way that hut does, and narrate the difference between what the audience is told and what a character inside the space could possibly know. This is direct preparation for the dramatic-irony scene in your own Bard’s Hall story.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Working solo, choose or arrange a small space and place one ordinary-looking object in it: a letter, a bag, a piece of furniture, anything that can quietly hold a secret.
2
Decide what that object actually means or contains, something none of the characters in the room currently suspect, and state it aloud directly to your recording, as if speaking to the audience alone.
3
Then act out one character entering the room and treating the object as ordinary: sitting near it, ignoring it, or discussing something unrelated right beside it.
4
Narrate one sentence naming exactly what the audience now knows that the character in the room does not, and why that gap matters to the story.
5
Save your capture and submit it, following the submission instructions below.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A short (about 60 to 90 second) recording or capture of your staged scene, in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a screen or video recording with narration, an audio recording paired with a few photos or sketches, or a short written script with a brief description if recording is not possible. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
The audience is told the hidden meaning of the object clearly, before the character enters the room.
☐
The character in the room treats the object as ordinary, with no sign of the knowledge the audience has.
☐
One sentence names the gap between what the audience knows and what the character knows.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can support it by modeling the audience-address line quickly (naming a secret directly to camera before acting out a scene) before students try it themselves. Common sticking point: students have the character react to the secret without meaning to; redirect them to the completion criteria above, which requires the character to treat the object as completely ordinary.
