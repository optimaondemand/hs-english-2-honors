Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
No advocate faces a friendly Council. In this activity, working solo, you build an evidence wall for your case and then attack it yourself, posing the hardest, fairest questions an opposing advocate would actually ask, and answering each one aloud, unscripted, in under thirty seconds. The goal is to find the weak point in your own brief before the Council does, while you still have time to fix it.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Set up or return to your evidence wall: a physical or digital space where the key evidence from your brief is posted where you can see it (note cards, printed quotes, or a whiteboard).
2
Write three challenge questions, in your own words, that an opposing advocate would genuinely ask about your weakest points, not softball questions you already know how to answer easily. Post each one next to the evidence it targets.
3
Standing at your evidence wall, read the first challenge question aloud, then answer it aloud, unscripted, in under thirty seconds.
4
Repeat for the second and third questions, keeping each answer under thirty seconds. Do not rehearse your answers word for word beforehand; the point is thinking on your feet.
5
Record the full sequence, all three questions and answers, and save it, following the submission instructions below.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A short recording (about three to five minutes total) showing your evidence wall and capturing all three questions and answers, in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a video recording, an audio recording, or, if recording is not possible, your three written questions along with a written summary of how you answered each one. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
Three challenge questions are posted, each naming a real weak point in the case, not an easy question.
☐
Each question is answered aloud, unscripted, in under thirty seconds.
☐
At least one answer draws directly on the Response section of the brief.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can support it by posing one hostile question aloud to the class and modeling a thirty-second unscripted answer before students write their own. Common sticking point: students write questions that are too easy, ones they can already answer perfectly. Redirect them to imagine the harshest, fairest question the opposing advocate could actually raise, and remind them that finding the weak point now is safer than a Council finding it first.
