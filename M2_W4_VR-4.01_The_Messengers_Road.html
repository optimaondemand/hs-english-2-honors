Solo VR Activity 4.01
The Messenger’s Road
Solo VR Activity · Week 4 · Completion-graded
Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
This week a messenger travels from Corinth to Thebes carrying what he believes is good news, and ends up delivering the most devastating revelation in the play, entirely by accident and entirely out of kindness. In this activity, working solo, you will walk that same road yourself, rehearsing the moment good intentions carry catastrophe without anyone meaning it to.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Working solo, choose or arrange a walking path, a hallway, a room-to-room route, or an outdoor path, to represent the road the Corinthian messenger traveled to reach Thebes.
2
As you walk, speak aloud, in your own words, the news you believe you are carrying: that Corinth wants Oedipus for its king, now that Polybus has died of old age.
3
Partway along your route, pause and speak the moment your good news turns: say aloud, in your own words, the messenger’s own belief that he is doing Oedipus a kindness, right before that kindness destroys him.
4
Finish your walk and record a brief spoken reflection: describe a real or imagined moment when someone’s genuine good intentions still caused harm, and what that tells you about the messenger’s role in this scene.
5
Save your capture and submit it, following the submission instructions below.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A short (about 60 to 90 second) recording or capture of your walk, in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a screen or video recording with narration, an audio recording paired with a few photos or sketches of your route, or a short written script with a brief description if recording is not possible. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
The recording includes both the “good news” you speak at the start and the turn toward the devastating revelation partway through.
☐
The reflection names a real or imagined instance of good intentions still causing harm.
☐
The reflection connects explicitly to the messenger’s stated purpose, to give Oedipus pleasure, not to harm him.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can support it by modeling the tone shift quickly (cheerful news-bearer, then unwitting bearer of catastrophe, in a 30-second demo) before students try it themselves. Common sticking point: students play the messenger as intentionally cruel or manipulative; redirect them to the completion criteria above, which requires the reflection to connect to the messenger’s genuine, stated intent to please the king, not to harm him.
