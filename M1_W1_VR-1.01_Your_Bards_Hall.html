Solo VR Activity 1.01
Your Bard’s Hall
Solo VR Activity · Week 1 · Completion-graded
Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
Every telling needs a space. Before you write a word of your own story, you will imagine and arrange the place where it will someday be performed: a hall, a fireside, or a modern equivalent where a teller stands before a listening audience. This is the first step toward The Bard’s Hall, your Module 1 project, and it asks you to think like the poets who first performed the Odyssey aloud.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Working solo, choose a space of your own to represent a place where stories are told aloud: a great hall, a fireside, a porch, a modern stage, or any equivalent you find fitting.
2
Arrange the space with a listening audience in mind: where the teller stands or sits, where listeners gather, and what makes this spot a good place to hold a room’s attention.
3
Walk through the space and describe it aloud: where the teller will stand, where the audience will sit, and one detail that will help your future telling of The Bard’s Hall land well in this spot.
4
Name, in one or two sentences of your walkthrough, why this space suits an oral telling, the way the Phaeacian court suited the bard Demodocus’s song of Troy.
5
Save your capture and submit it, following the submission instructions below.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A short (about 60 to 90 second) recording or capture of your spatial walkthrough, in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a screen or video recording with narration, an audio recording paired with a few photos or sketches of the space, or a short written description with images if recording is not possible. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
A space has been chosen and arranged with a teller’s spot and a listening area.
☐
The recording walks through the space and describes where the teller and audience will be.
☐
The recording states one reason this space suits a spoken telling.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can support it by showing one or two quick real-world examples of spaces built for a listening audience (a classroom reading corner, a campfire circle, a small stage) before students choose their own, and by reminding students that any device-agnostic capture is acceptable so long as it shows the space and the walkthrough. Common sticking point: students over-build the space instead of describing it; redirect them to the 60 to 90 second target and the completion criteria above.
