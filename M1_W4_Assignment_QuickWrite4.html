📖 WRITING FOCUS
The epic simile and figurative language
Narrative craft · builds toward your Module 1 project, The Bard’s Hall
In Lesson 4.03 you studied how Homer stretches a single comparison into a whole second scene, the way Odysseus’s weeping is compared to a woman mourning her fallen husband. Now you build a scene of your own Bard’s Hall tale around one long comparison of that kind.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Write a scene (150 to 250 words) from your Bard’s Hall tale that includes one epic simile: a comparison, introduced with “as” or “like,” that keeps building past its first image into a small, fully imagined second scene.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How can one long, carefully built comparison make a reader feel the true size of a moment?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Choose a moment of strong feeling from your Bard’s Hall tale: fear, relief, grief, pride, longing, or anything else that hits hard.
2
Write one epic simile into that moment: begin with “as” or “like,” then keep building the comparison for at least a full sentence before returning to your scene. Do not stop at a quick, one-line comparison.
3
Include one parallel series of three or more matched items somewhere else in the scene, and choose precise words over vague ones throughout.
4
Work in at least two of this week’s Word Study words (suppliant, lofty, minstrel, muse, prowess, nimbly), used accurately.
5
Reread once. Ask yourself: does my comparison build a real second scene, or does it stop too soon? Extend it if it stops too soon.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
One epic simile builds a fully imagined second scene, not just a quick comparison.
☐
One parallel series of three or more matched items appears clearly in the scene.
☐
At least two of this week’s Word Study words are used accurately.
☐
The writing is 150 to 250 words and reads clearly, in complete sentences.
