🎯 THE QUESTION THIS MODULE ASKS
When a community suffers, what does it owe its king, and what does the king owe it?
This week the question turns backward, into Oedipus’s own birth. A man who has spent his whole life trying to outrun a single prophecy learns that the parents he fled were never his own, brought to him by a messenger who means nothing but kindness. Meanwhile the one person on stage who has just understood the whole truth, his wife, tries to stop him and fails. Watch closely who is kind, who is honest, and how kindness and honesty can each, entirely on their own, still lead somewhere terrible.
📖 THIS WEEK’S READING — BY SCENE LANDMARK
The Corinthian messenger through Jocasta’s exit and the chorus’s hopeful ode
Scene landmarks to watch for: a messenger arriving from Corinth with news of King Polybus’s death; the brief, false relief this brings Oedipus and Jocasta; Oedipus’s remaining dread of his mother, Merope; the messenger, trying only to help, revealing that Oedipus was never Polybus’s true son; the detail of the infant’s pierced and pinned feet; Jocasta’s plea that Oedipus stop searching, and her exit into the palace; and the chorus’s ode, singing hopefully about a mysterious, perhaps even divine, parentage for their king. Read the full text free at Project Gutenberg #31 if you want it alongside the passages quoted in your lessons.
📋 WHAT’S IN THIS WEEK
Lesson
4.01 · Good News That Isn’t
The messenger’s news of Polybus’s death, the relief that follows, and the revelation that undoes it: how a single new fact can change what every earlier fact was proving.
Lesson
4.02 · The Queen Who Understood
Jocasta’s plea, her exit, and the moment Oedipus reads her fear entirely backward. The play’s cruelest use of dramatic irony.
Lesson
4.03 · The Strongest Case Against You
A writing-technique lesson: stating the opposing verdict’s best argument fairly, then answering it. Builds toward Quick Write 12.
Grammar & Word Study
4.GV · Concession Language
Phrases like “while it is true that,” “granted,” and “even if” that let a writer give ground fairly without losing the argument, plus this week’s Word Study words.
Composition
Quick Write 12 · Steelman the Other Side
State the opposing verdict’s strongest argument as fairly as you can, then answer it. Feeder 4 of 8 toward the Trial of Oedipus.
Recitation
Your Q2 Recitation launches this week
This is the natural week for it: you have just watched two people fail to hear each other clearly, and you are about to spend the next several weeks learning to speak the play’s own words carefully and aloud. The Recitation pages (a passage menu drawn from the priest’s plague-plea or the closing chorus, practice guidance, and the due date) live at the module level. Ungraded practice begins next week; the recording is due in Week 8.
VR
Solo VR Activity 4.01 · The Messenger’s Road
Walk the road the Corinthian messenger traveled, and rehearse how good intentions can carry catastrophe without meaning to.
VR
Solo VR Activity 4.02 · The Courtroom Takes Shape
Begin staking out the tribunal space, judge’s seat, advocate’s floor, and the accused’s place, where you will present your closing argument in Week 9.
