Solo VR Activity 5.01
The Teller’s Voice
Solo VR Activity · Week 5 · Completion-graded
Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
This week Odysseus became the teller of his own story, at a tense moment, in front of an audience deciding whether to trust him. In this activity, working solo, you will rehearse telling a tense scene from your own Bard’s Hall story out loud, in-space, paying attention to your own voice: pacing, tone, and the moments where you slow down or speed up. This also gives you a running start on your recitation, which is now in its practice window.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Working solo, choose or arrange a space where you can speak aloud without interruption: a quiet room, a corner set up as your own telling-space, or any comfortable equivalent.
2
Choose one tense scene from your own Bard’s Hall story, either something already drafted (Quick Write 1 through 5) or a moment you plan to write soon.
3
Tell the scene aloud twice. The first time, tell it quickly and flatly. The second time, slow down at the most tense moment and let your voice change, the way a teller building suspense would.
4
Narrate one sentence, out loud, about which version felt more like a real telling, and why.
5
Save your capture and submit it, following the submission instructions below.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A short (about 60 to 90 second) recording or capture of your two tellings and your closing comparison sentence, in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a screen or video recording with narration, an audio recording, or a short written script with a brief description of the pacing changes if recording is not possible. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
The same scene is told twice, once flatly and once with a deliberate change in pacing or tone.
☐
One sentence names which version felt more like a real telling, and why.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can support it by modeling the flat-then-paced contrast quickly (telling one sentence two ways) before students try it themselves. Common sticking point: students rush through both tellings the same way; redirect them to slow down noticeably at the tense moment in the second telling, since the contrast is the point of the activity.
