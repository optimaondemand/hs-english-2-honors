Rehearsing the Opening
Solo VR Activity · Week 7 · Completion-graded
Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
You have written your first drafted argument section this week. Before your closing argument in Week 9, you need to know how your case sounds out loud, not just how it reads on the page. In this activity, working solo, you will return to the advocate’s floor you marked out in Week 4 and deliver the opening minute of your brief exactly as you intend to open the real case, then hear it back.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Working solo, return to the tribunal space you staked out in Week 4 and stand on the advocate’s floor, facing the judge’s seat.
2
Choose your opening minute: your Issue and Verdict from Quick Write 14, spoken as you would say them aloud to open your case.
3
Deliver the opening aloud, from memory or a short note card, without reading it word for word off a full script. Speak at the pace you would use before a real audience.
4
Play the recording back once. Note one thing that sounded stronger out loud than you expected, and one thing you want to revise before Week 9.
5
Save your recording and submit it, following the instructions below.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A short (about 60 to 90 second) recording of your delivered opening, in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a video recording, an audio-only recording, or, if recording truly is not possible, a written script plus a short note on how the read-aloud practice felt and what you would revise. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
The recording is delivered from the advocate’s floor and includes both your Issue and your Verdict.
☐
The delivery is spoken aloud, not read silently or typed only, at a natural speaking pace.
☐
One strength and one planned revision are named, in the recording or in a short written note.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can support it by reminding students the goal is delivery, not memorization; a note card with the Issue and Verdict written out is a completely acceptable prompt. Common sticking point: students read their Quick Write 14 outline word for word in a flat voice; redirect them to speak it as though a real listener were in front of them, glancing at notes rather than reading continuously.
