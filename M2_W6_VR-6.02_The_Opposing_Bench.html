Solo VR Activity 6.02
The Opposing Bench
Solo VR Activity · Week 6 · Completion-graded
Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
Your Response, on paper, is only as strong as the opposing case you actually imagined when you wrote it. In this activity, working solo, you will physically stand where the opposing advocate would stand and argue their strongest case against your own verdict, out loud, in their voice. The advocate who has genuinely stood at the other bench writes a far better Response than the one who only guessed at what it might feel like from a distance.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Working solo, mark two spots a few steps apart: your bench, where you argue your own verdict, and the opposing bench, facing it, where the advocate arguing against you would stand.
2
Standing at your own bench, briefly say your verdict-thesis aloud (from Quick Write 14), so you have a clear target in the air for the opposition to argue against.
3
Physically move to the opposing bench. Standing there, in the voice of the advocate arguing the opposing verdict, deliver their best 45 seconds against you: their strongest claim and at least one real piece of evidence, spoken as though you genuinely believed it.
4
Return to your own bench. Say, or write in your Organizer, one honest sentence naming what the opposing argument got right, the part your Response in Quick Write 14 needs to answer more directly.
5
Save your recording and submit it, following the instructions below.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A short (about 60 to 90 second) recording or capture showing your move from your bench to the opposing bench, your 45-second opposing argument, and your one-sentence return note, in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a screen or video recording, an audio recording with a couple of photos marking the two benches, or a written script of the opposing argument plus your return note if recording is not possible. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
The opposing argument is delivered from the physical opposing bench, not from your own.
☐
The opposing argument states a real claim and at least one specific piece of evidence, not just “you are wrong.”
☐
A follow-up note names one specific thing your own Response should address more directly.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can support it by reminding students, before they move to the opposing bench, that the goal is to argue the strongest real version of the other side, not a weak version built to lose. Common sticking point: students deliver a strawman opposing argument with no real evidence attached; redirect them to the completion criteria above, which requires at least one specific piece of evidence, not just disagreement.
