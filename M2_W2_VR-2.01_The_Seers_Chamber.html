Solo VR Activity 2.01
The Seer’s Chamber
Solo VR Activity · Week 2 · Completion-graded
Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
Oedipus and Tiresias never move closer together in this scene. A king who can see and a prophet who cannot spend the whole confrontation talking across a gap neither one crosses. In this activity, working solo, you will build that space physically, two facing positions with real distance between them, and speak the king’s side of the accusation the way Oedipus hears it: as an insult from a man who should be loyal to him. This is direct preparation for staging the courtroom itself later in the module.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Working solo in your chosen Location, set two chairs or two marked spots facing each other, with a real, deliberate gap between them: at least several steps of open floor. This is the seer’s chamber.
2
Stand or sit at the Oedipus position. Speak this line aloud as Oedipus reacting to it, with the anger and disbelief of a king who feels betrayed: “Thou art the man, thou the accursed polluter of this land.”
3
Notice the gap between the two positions as you speak. Nothing closes it. Narrate one sentence out loud about what that unclosed physical distance says about the two men in this scene.
4
Save your capture and submit it, following the submission instructions below. You will return to this same space next time to record the line from Tiresias’s side.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A short (about 45 to 60 second) recording or capture of your staged chamber and Oedipus’s line, in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a screen or video recording with narration, an audio recording paired with a photo or sketch of your two-position layout, or a short written script with a brief description if recording is not possible. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
Two facing positions with a real, visible gap between them are staged.
☐
Tiresias’s accusation is spoken from the Oedipus position, as an insult heard in anger.
☐
One sentence names what the unclosed gap between the two positions communicates.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can support it by demonstrating how to mark a real physical gap between two positions before students set up their own space. Common sticking point: students read the line flatly instead of playing the emotion Oedipus actually feels in the moment; redirect them to imagine genuine anger and betrayal, since the follow-up activity depends on that contrast being clear.
