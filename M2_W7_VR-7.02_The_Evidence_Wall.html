Solo VR Activity 7.02
The Evidence Wall
Solo VR Activity · Week 7 · Completion-graded
Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
Your evidence currently lives scattered across weeks of notes: the Evidence Bank, the Research Log, this week’s draft. In this activity, working solo, you will build a physical evidence wall, arranging your quotes and sources spatially by which argument each one supports, then walk an imagined opposing counsel through your case aloud. Seeing your evidence laid out in space, rather than buried in a document, often reveals gaps or connections a page of text hides.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Working solo, choose a surface or space you can arrange items on: a wall, a table, a whiteboard, or a VR canvas or Drawpad if your platform supports one.
2
Write each piece of evidence you plan to use, your play quotes and your JStor source, on its own card, sticky note, or labeled block.
3
Arrange the cards into clusters, one cluster per argument in your outline, so the wall shows which evidence supports which claim, and where a claim has only one thin piece of evidence behind it.
4
Speaking to an imagined opposing counsel, walk the wall aloud: point to (or gesture toward) each cluster in turn, name the argument it supports, and note honestly if any cluster looks thinner than the rest.
5
Record the walkthrough, save it, and submit it, following the instructions below.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A short (about 90 second to 2 minute) recording of your evidence-wall walkthrough, in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a video recording showing the wall while you narrate, an audio recording paired with a photo of the arranged cards, or a written description of the wall’s layout plus a short written walkthrough if recording is not possible. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
Evidence is arranged in clusters that visibly correspond to the arguments in your outline.
☐
Every cluster is named aloud in the recording, along with the argument it supports.
☐
At least one honest observation about a thin or missing cluster is spoken aloud, even if no cluster actually looks thin.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can support students with limited materials by suggesting a simple substitute (index cards, sticky notes, or even a shared document with movable text boxes work as well as a physical wall). Common sticking point: students arrange evidence chronologically (in the order it appears in the play) instead of by argument; redirect them to group by which claim each piece supports, since that is the organization their actual brief needs.
