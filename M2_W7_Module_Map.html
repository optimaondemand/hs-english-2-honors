🎯 THE QUESTION THIS WEEK SHARPENS
You have a standard, a verdict, and an outline. What turns that outline into a brief a skeptical reader actually believes?
Last week you built the architecture of your case. This week you fill it in, in full prose, and that is where most arguments actually succeed or fail: not in having the right evidence, but in how that evidence is placed on the page and how honestly it is credited. This week you write one complete argument section of your brief, quotations integrated and cited the way a professional would cite them.
📖 THIS WEEK — A DRAFTING WEEK, NOT A READING WEEK
No new scenes. Reread anything you plan to cite before you cite it.
Before you quote a line in your brief, go back and reread it in context. A quotation lifted from memory, or from a note you copied down in Week 2, can drift from the actual wording without you noticing. This week’s work rewards precision, not speed.
🔗 Reread Oedipus the King at Project Gutenberg (#31)
⚖️ YOUR MODULE PROJECT — THE TRIAL OF OEDIPUS
You are turning last week’s Issue, Verdict, and outline into real prose. This week’s Quick Write asks you to draft one complete argument section: a claim, integrated and correctly cited evidence, reasoning, and a sentence anticipating the counterclaim you will fully answer later. Every quotation you use from here forward should earn its place on the page, not simply decorate it.
📋 WHAT’S IN THIS WEEK
Lesson
7.01 · The Quote That Earns Its Place
Choosing the sharp short quote over the long block quote, and blending it into your own sentence so it does real work instead of decorating the page.
Lesson
7.02 · Citing Like a Professional
MLA citation for this brief: a verse play quoted in prose, and a scholarly journal article from JStor, with a full Works Cited entry modeled for each.
Grammar & Word Study
7.GV · Citation Mechanics and Conciseness
The punctuation of citation (where the period goes, question marks inside quotations), cutting throat-clearing openers, and this week’s Word Study words.
