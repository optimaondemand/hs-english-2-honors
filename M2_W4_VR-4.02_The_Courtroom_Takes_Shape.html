The Courtroom Takes Shape
Solo VR Activity · Week 4 · Completion-graded
Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
In Week 9 you will present your closing argument in the Trial of Oedipus, standing before the Council of Thebes. This week, five weeks early, you begin literally building that space, wherever you do your VR work, so you have a physical anchor to return to as your case develops. In this activity, working solo, you will stake out three zones and stand in each one before the room is ever used for its real purpose.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Working solo, choose a space and identify three distinct zones within it: the judge’s seat (the Council of Thebes), the advocate’s floor (where you will stand to argue your case), and the accused’s place (where Oedipus, empty for now, will eventually stand).
2
Mark or arrange each zone with an object, a taped line, or a simple label so the three areas stay distinct as you move between them.
3
Stand on the advocate’s floor and speak one sentence stating your current verdict, exactly as you would announce it to open a real case.
4
Walk to the accused’s place, look back at where you just stood, and record one sentence describing what it might feel like to be the one judged in that room.
5
Record a short walkthrough naming and narrating all three zones in order, then save and submit it following the instructions below.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A short (about 60 to 90 second) recording or capture of your three-zone walkthrough, in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a screen or video recording with narration, an audio recording paired with a few photos of your zones, or a short written script with a brief description if recording is not possible. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
All three zones, the judge’s seat, the advocate’s floor, and the accused’s place, are clearly named and shown in the walkthrough.
☐
A spoken sentence stating the current verdict, delivered from the advocate’s floor, is included.
☐
A reflection sentence spoken from the accused’s place is included.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can help students with limited space brainstorm zones (three chairs or three taped floor spots are enough to establish the three areas). Common sticking point: students skip standing in the accused’s place because it feels awkward or unnecessary; note that this step is required, since part of an advocate’s skill is understanding the position of the person being judged, not only the position of the person arguing.
