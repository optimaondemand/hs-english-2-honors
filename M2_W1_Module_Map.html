🎯 THE QUESTION THIS MODULE ASKS
When a community suffers, what does it owe its king, and what does the king owe it?
Module 1 asked what a community owes an absent king, and what he owes it when he returns. Module 2 sharpens the question until it hurts: what does a community do when its own king may be the very source of its sickness? This week, the play opens with a city stricken by plague, begging the king who once saved it to save it again.
📖 THIS WEEK’S READING — FREE TO READ ONLINE
The play’s opening, through Oedipus’s proclamation and curse
Scene landmarks to watch for: the priest’s plea (the suppliants gathered before the palace), Creon’s news from Delphi (Apollo’s word on the plague), and Oedipus’s proclamation and curse (his public vow to find Laius’s killer). We refer to these moments by name, not by line number.
▶
Oedipus the King is public domain and free to read: Project Gutenberg, Sophocles, trans. F. Storr (it is the first play in the volume). Read as much or as little online as you like; the key passages are also quoted directly in this week’s lessons.
⚖️ YOUR MODULE PROJECT — THE TRIAL OF OEDIPUS
This module builds toward a single project: Oedipus stands trial before the Council of Thebes. You will serve as an advocate who takes a position, guilty, not guilty, or a defensible verdict in between, and argue it in a formal brief, answering the strongest case against you, and citing both the play and outside research. You will close with a recorded closing argument. You do not have to decide your verdict yet. Each week’s Quick Write builds one piece of the case, starting this week with your first, provisional verdict.
