🎮 DESIGN FOCUS
A world declared, and grounded in real research
The Designer’s Table · feeds your world/lore narrative and your eventual game
You met all four worlds in Sprint 1’s World Tour. Now the choice becomes real: you commit to one world for the rest of the year, take your first real look at it through a scholarly source, and put your AI-generated first impression through an honest check. This sprint produces the world your game will stand on.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Declare one of the four worlds from the World Tour as the setting for your year-long game. Log your first real JStor source on that world. Use an AI tool to get oriented, then check its claims against JStor. Then draft a short world/lore document establishing your game’s setting, grounded in what you actually verified, not in the unverified AI summary.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
What is the story your world’s real history already argues, before your game adds a single rule to it?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Declare your world. Choose one of the four worlds from the Sprint 1 World Tour: Bronze Age / Homeric Greece, Classical Athens, Elizabethan England, or Jazz-Age America. This is the world your Designer’s Table game builds in for the rest of the year, even if the course reads that world’s text later.
2
Find and log JStor source #1. Using the JStor Research Tutorial lesson, find one real scholarly source about your chosen period. Log three things: the full citation, the specific claim you are borrowing from it, and one sentence on how that claim will shape your game’s world.
3
Ask AI, then verify. Ask an AI tool (Microsoft Copilot through your school account, or another common tool you have) to summarize your chosen period in a paragraph. Then check every specific claim it made against JStor. Write down what it got wrong, oversimplified, or invented.
4
Draft your world/lore document (200–400 words). Using your verified research, not the unverified AI summary, establish where and when your world is set, what its people value or fear, and at least one real historical detail from your JStor source that will shape a rule, object, or story beat in your eventual game.
5
Log your AI use. Add one complete row to your AI-Use Log for the period-summary step above: what you asked, what it gave you, how you verified it, and what you changed.
⚠️ AI-USE REMINDER
Any claim the AI gave you that you could not verify on JStor does not belong in your lore document. Cut it, no matter how useful it sounded. Your AI-Use Log entry for this sprint is part of the submission, not optional extra credit.
