📖 WRITING FOCUS
Dramatic irony and the gap between reader and character
Narrative craft · builds toward your Module 1 project, The Bard’s Hall
This week you read a stretch of the Odyssey where the reader knows the beggar is really Odysseus long before most of the characters around him do. In Lesson 7.03 you practiced building that same gap into a scene of your own. Now you revise a real scene from your Bard’s Hall tale so the reader is let in on something a character in that scene does not yet know.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Revise or write a scene from your Bard’s Hall tale (200 to 300 words) so that the reader clearly knows something a character in the scene does not: a hidden identity, a secret plan, a danger just out of sight, or a piece of news that has not reached them yet. Keep every pronoun in the scene pointing to exactly one person.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
What does your reader already know in this scene that one of your characters does not, and how do you let that gap show without ever explaining it?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Find a scene in your Bard’s Hall draft where one character could plausibly not know something another character, or the reader, already knows. If nothing fits yet, write a brand-new scene using the same idea.
2
State the hidden fact clearly for the reader, early in the scene, the way Athena’s warning in Book 15 tells the reader about the suitors’ ambush before Telemachus ever sails past it.
3
Let a character in the scene speak or act as though that fact were not true, without any narrator line that explains the irony out loud.
4
Check every pronoun: make sure “he,” “she,” or “they” can only point to one person, renaming a character where needed. Work in at least two of this week’s Word Study words, used accurately.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
The reader clearly knows something a character in the scene does not.
☐
The irony is shown through the character’s own words or actions, not explained by the narrator.
☐
Every pronoun in the scene points to exactly one clear person.
☐
At least two of this week’s Word Study words are used accurately.
☐
The writing is 200 to 300 words.
📊 HOW IT’S SCORED
Criteria Full credit Partial Pts
Dramatic irony built
5 pts		 The reader clearly knows more than a character does, shown through action or dialogue, never explained outright. The gap is present but unclear, or the irony is explained rather than shown. 5
Pronoun clarity
3 pts		 Every pronoun in the scene points to exactly one person; agreement is correct throughout. One or more pronoun references are unclear or mismatched. 3
Word Study & length
2 pts		 At least two Word Study words used accurately; 200 to 300 words. Fewer than two Word Study words, or off the length target. 2
Total: 10 pts
