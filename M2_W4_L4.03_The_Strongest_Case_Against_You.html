Lesson 4.03
The Strongest Case Against You
Lesson · Week 4 · Writing Technique
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
A case that never answers its best opposing argument is not actually a strong case. It just has not been tested yet.
Every advocate arguing a verdict on Oedipus has to face a hard fact: the other side has real arguments too, and a skeptical reader already knows it. Today you learn how to state the strongest version of the opposing verdict, fairly and forcefully, and then answer it. This is the exact move you will make in Quick Write 12, and it is one of the most persuasive things a writer can do, because a reader who sees you have already considered their best objection trusts everything else you say more.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to define counterclaim and rebuttal and explain why an unanswered counterclaim weakens even well-supported writing.
2
I will be able to distinguish a fairly stated counterclaim from a weak, easily dismissed one.
3
I will be able to draft a rebuttal that either answers a counterclaim with evidence and reasoning, or concedes part of it honestly.
📚 PART 1 · THE ARGUMENT YOU HAVEN’T ANSWERED YET
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Think of a strong disagreement you have had. Did you ever seriously consider the best version of the other person’s point, not just the easiest version to dismiss? What happened? (No wrong answer.)

Two new terms, one habit

A counterclaim is the strongest reasonable argument against your own position, stated in its best form, not its weakest one. A rebuttal is your answer to that counterclaim: either you refute it with evidence and reasoning, or you concede part of it honestly and explain why your verdict still holds anyway. Skipping the counterclaim does not make your case stronger. It just means a skeptical reader supplies the counterclaim themselves, unanswered, and concludes you never considered it.

📚 PART 2 · THE STRONGEST VERSION, NOT THE EASIEST ONE

A weak or mocked counterclaim convinces no one, including you. If you state the other side’s argument in a form no reasonable person would actually make, a careful reader notices immediately, and your whole essay looks less trustworthy for it. The goal is the opposite: state the other side’s best, most reasonable case, as if you believed it yourself, before you answer it.

📝 THE STRONGEST CASE FOR EACH VERDICT
STEELMANNING “NOT GUILTY”
Oedipus could not have known the stranger at the crossroads was his father. He had already tried to flee the very prophecy that trapped him, leaving Corinth specifically to avoid it. The oracle set this trap in motion before he was even born, through no choice of his own.
STEELMANNING “GUILTY”
Whatever Oedipus knew or did not know, he killed a man in anger at a crossroads. His own rashness in that moment, and his refusal now to stop asking questions once Jocasta begged him to, are choices that belong to him, not to fate.

Notice that both versions are genuinely persuasive on their own. Neither one is a strawman. That is exactly what makes them worth answering instead of ignoring.

✍️ TRY IT YOURSELF, IN YOUR NOTEBOOK, BEFORE YOU LOOK AT THE MODEL BELOW
Write the single strongest sentence you can construct FOR the verdict you do NOT currently hold, as if you believed it yourself. Do this in your notebook first.
Compare your work: a model steelman and rebuttal (open only after you have written your own)
Steelman (arguing FOR “not guilty,” from a writer who ultimately argues guilty): “Oedipus had no way to know the man he struck at the crossroads was Laius, and he had already left Corinth specifically to keep the prophecy from coming true.”
Rebuttal: Even granting that Oedipus could not have known Laius’s identity, intent is not the only standard by which Thebes can judge a killing. An armed man cut down in anger at a crossroads still requires an answer, whatever the killer knew at the time.
📚 PART 3 · CONCEDE WHAT MUST BE CONCEDED

Not every counterclaim needs to be defeated outright. Sometimes the strongest rebuttal concedes a real, true piece of the other side’s argument, and then explains why that concession does not sink your overall verdict. Giving honest ground on one point, then reframing why your case still stands, often reads as more trustworthy than trying to win every single point. You will learn the specific language for doing this cleanly in tomorrow’s grammar lesson.

⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND COUNTERCLAIMS
An AI tool can generate an opposing argument for almost any position in seconds. What it is weak at is generating the single strongest version specific to this exact case, grounded in the actual facts of the messenger scene and Jocasta’s exit, rather than a generic argument about guilt and innocence. If you use an AI tool to brainstorm a counterclaim, check its argument against the actual text and sharpen it yourself before you write your rebuttal.
