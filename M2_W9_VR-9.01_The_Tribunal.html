Solo VR Activity · Week 9 · Completion-graded
Note for build planning: this page describes what you will do and why. Step-by-step directions for your specific device or platform will be added later. For now, focus on the goal below.
🎯 OBJECTIVE
Since Week 4 you have been staking out a courtroom space of your own: somewhere that could plausibly stand in for a tribunal hearing the case of a king. This week, that space becomes the Tribunal of Thebes. In this activity, working solo, you will stand before the bench you have built and deliver, aloud, the closing argument you rehearsed in Lesson 9.02, then gather or supply a verdict and the reasoning behind it, the way a real tribunal would.
🗺️ WHAT YOU’LL DO
1
Return to (or reassemble, if needed) the courtroom or tribunal space you staked out between Weeks 4 and 8: a bench, a place for you to stand as the advocate, and a sense of who is present to hear you.
2
Where it is possible, invite one to three listeners, family members, peers, or classmates, to serve as your Council bench, either live or by recording your argument for them to review afterward.
3
Standing before the bench, deliver your full recorded closing argument exactly as you rehearsed it in Lesson 9.02, including the final sentence you practiced until it landed.
4
If you have real listeners, ask them to render a verdict and give one sentence of reasoning, and record their response as part of your capture.
5
If no listeners are available, record both of these yourself instead: the verdict you honestly believe a fair, uninvolved Council would reach after hearing your argument, and the strongest reason that Council might rule against you anyway.
6
Save your capture and submit it, following the submission instructions below.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
A recording or capture of your tribunal, in whatever device-agnostic form you have available: a video recording of your delivered closing argument and the resulting verdict, an audio recording paired with a few photos of your staged space, or a written account of the closing argument and verdict if recording is not possible. Save it in your ELA10 OneDrive folder and submit through Canvas. This recorded closing argument is also the one you submit as part of your Trial of Oedipus final project; see that project page for full submission instructions, the rubric, and the due date.
✅ COMPLETION CRITERIA
☐
The full closing argument is delivered aloud, standing at the staged tribunal space.
☐
A verdict and one sentence of reasoning are captured, either from real listeners or recorded by the student.
☐
If self-recorded, both the likely verdict AND the strongest reason a fair Council might rule against it are present, not only one.
☐
The file is saved and submitted by the due date. (Completion credit; no rubric score.)
🏗️ FACILITATOR NOTE
This is a solo, asynchronous activity; there is no separate live version. A live teacher can support it by helping a student who has no available listeners understand that a small, informal audience, even one family member, or simply the recording device itself, is enough; the point is delivering the argument aloud before a real or imagined bench, not the size of the crowd. Common sticking point: students who record their own verdict tend to supply only the outcome they want, skipping the strongest reason a fair Council might rule against them. Redirect students to the completion criteria above, which requires both.
